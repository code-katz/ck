Three comments, and what each taught:

1. **"too jargony"**, on the label "The chain:" in Problem and root-cause chain. Claude rewrote the whole section in plain words. Will meant the label. The content went back; the label became "Why, asked three times:". Lesson: a comment is a request to propose, not a licence to change. 2. **"this bullet point starts at #3, why?"**, on Toni's section. Toni had numbered the answers to River's five questions; River moved answers 1 and 2 into Market context, so Toni's section began at 3. Fixed in the document, and in the script: contributors answer in prose under short lead-ins, with no numbering.
3. **"What do these handoff briefs mean? What am I to do with them?"** The contributors' notes to each other, pasted into the document by River. Nothing for the author to do with them. Removed from the document, and from the script: no hand-off briefs in the assembled document; a contributor's question for the author goes to Open questions.

The structural finding: two Claudes on one document. Will sent each comment to Claude from the page. A comment sent to Claude wakes a cloud session (this one), not the Claude Code session on the Mac that wrote the file. So the cloud session changed the page and replied in the threads, the Mac session changed the file when Will typed "done" there, and each had to be told what the other had done; one of Will's answers reached the wrong one. Will's decision: he and Clare work locally, so the review loop now assumes it. The banner's steps say not to send comments to Claude; the local session reads them at "done", proposes one change per comment in chat, applies only what the author approves, republishes once, and leaves the threads for the author to resolve on the page.

What changed in the plugin (0.1.1): `skills/review-page` rewritten around propose-then-apply and the local session; the four banner steps in both renderers and the gallery contract; `opportunity-draft.js` with prose answers and no hand-off briefs; README.
