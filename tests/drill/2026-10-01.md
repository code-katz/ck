What the fixes did: the renderer script removed the hand-built review page (102k to 15k); the memo caps removed the synthesis loop (234 turns to 7); the write-once rule cut the rewrite by two thirds (182k to 65k).

What is left: Fable 5.1 output tokens are three quarters of the cost. River's draft, lens, rewrite, and finalize together wrote about 139k output tokens at $50 per million, about $6.90 of the $8.05. The rewrite and the finalize still take 25 to 30 turns each even with the write-once instruction, so most of those tokens are reasoning, not document. The next lever is the tier for those two stages, which is Will's call under "Fable where it matters": the draft and the lens are judgment, the rewrite and the finalize are editing against a memo.

Will's decision, 2026-10-01: keep Fable. The rewrite and the finalize stay on River's tier. In his words, a more costly PRD is fine because it is the most important step of a product kickoff; $8 per PRD is the accepted cost, and the remaining levers are turn counts, not models.

Two more findings:

1. The panel inside `/ck:prd` returned yes-if from all three lenses for the third run in a row, so `panelFailedToDisagree` fired again. The question "Is this PRD ready for the author's review, and what would you change before it ships?" invites yes-if. A question that forces a stance is worth trying before changing anything else about the panel.
2. The harness: a detached run dies about three minutes after the session's turn ends, because the container is reclaimed when idle; a background tool command dies at ten minutes. A measurement longer than ten minutes needs a Monitor armed for its duration, which keeps the session awake. Two attempts were lost learning this (the first at the ten-minute limit after draft, validate, panel, and memo had finished for $4.13; the second at the container reclaim after draft and one revision).
